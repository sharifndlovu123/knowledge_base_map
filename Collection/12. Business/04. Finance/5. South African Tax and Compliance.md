# 05. South African Tax and Compliance

The registrations, taxes and filing duties of a small South African business, with verified rates and the official source for each.

> **Not from the library.** General reference material written to fill a gap in the books in this section.

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Figures below were checked against the official pages named in November 2026 and **change often** (budget each February, thresholds each year). Confirm on the official page, or with a registered tax practitioner, before acting. Anything I could not verify is left out or flagged.

Related: [[3. Accounting Basics|Accounting Basics]] (the records these duties depend on), [[1. Reading the Numbers|Reading the Numbers]], [[2. Cash Allocation|Cash Allocation]] (setting cash aside for tax).

## Business structures

| Structure | Legal status | Tax | Liability | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietor | Same person as owner | Owner's personal income tax | Unlimited | No registration; simplest |
| Partnership | Not separate entity (usually) | Partners taxed individually | Usually unlimited | Needs a written agreement |
| Private company (Pty) Ltd | Separate legal person | Company tax, then dividends tax on payouts | Limited (shareholders), but lenders may ask for sureties | Registered with CIPC; compliance burden |
| Close corporation | Older form | As company | Limited | New ones cannot be registered since the Companies Act 71 of 2008 took effect on 1 May 2011; existing ones continue and may convert to a company, but a company cannot convert to a CC (CIPC guidance, cipc.co.za; as of November 2026) |
| NPC / trust | Special purposes | Special rules | Varies | Outside this note |

- Choice factors: liability protection, tax at your income level, credibility with clients, admin cost, plans to raise funds or sell.
- Mistake: forming a company without thinking through how you will pay yourself (salary vs dividend, see below).

## CIPC: registration, annual returns, beneficial ownership
- CIPC (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission) registers companies and keeps the register. Source: cipc.co.za.
- Typical company steps: reserve a name, file the memorandum of incorporation, directors' details; receive registration number and certificate.
- Annual return: due **within 30 business days after the company's anniversary date** (the date of incorporation each year). The return must be accompanied by the beneficial ownership declaration and the securities register, and annual financial statements or a financial accountability supplement, as applicable. Source: CIPC page on incorporating beneficial ownership with annual return filings (cipc.co.za).
- Beneficial ownership (BO): CIPC's page states it enforces a "hard stop" from 15 April 2024: the annual return cannot be filed unless BO information is submitted and current. (CIPC documents give slightly different start dates for the hard stop; the current rule is what matters.) Newly incorporated entities must file BO within 10 business days of incorporation, per a CIPC notice.
- Financial reporting framework: under regulations 26-27 of the Companies Regulations, 2011, a company's public interest score decides the framework (score 350 or more: IFRS, or IFRS for SMEs if it meets the scoping criteria; 100 to under 350: IFRS, IFRS for SMEs or SA GAAP; under 100 and independently compiled: the same choices; internally compiled: its chosen standard). Whether the IASB's 2027 third edition has been formally recognised in South African regulation is not confirmed; see [[3. Accounting Basics|Accounting Basics]]. The score is under review by the dtic (parliamentary reply, 2026), so thresholds may change. As of November 2026.
- Consequences listed by CIPC: late-filing penalties, compliance notices, referral for deregistration.
- Annual return fees and company registration fees: left out. Only third-party sites quoted amounts and they disagreed; check the CIPC fee schedule on cipc.co.za.
- Mistake: ignoring a dormant company; it still has to file.

## Income tax (SARS)
**Company rates**, year of assessment ending 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. Source: SARS "Companies, Trusts and Small Business Corporations" page; the 2026 Budget left the rate unchanged.
- Standard company rate: **27%**.
- Small Business Corporation (SBC) scale:

| Taxable income (R) | Tax |
|---|---|
| 0 - 99 000 | 0% |
| 99 001 - 365 000 | 7% of amount above 99 000 |
| 365 001 - 550 000 | R18 620 + 21% above 365 000 |
| 550 001 and above | R57 470 + 27% above 550 000 |

- SBC conditions (SARS summary): shareholders are all natural persons, gross income not above R20 million, not a personal service company, not a holding company. Read the full definition on SARS before relying on it.
- Example: SBC with R450 000 taxable income: 18 620 + 21% x (450 000 - 365 000) = 18 620 + 17 850 = R36 470.
- Sole proprietors and partners are taxed under the **individual** progressive rates plus rebates. Rates for the 2027 tax year (1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027), as of November 2026. Source: SARS "Rates of tax for individuals":

| Taxable income (R) | Tax |
|---|---|
| 0 - 245 100 | 18% of taxable income |
| 245 101 - 383 100 | R44 118 + 26% above 245 100 |
| 383 101 - 530 200 | R79 998 + 31% above 383 100 |
| 530 201 - 695 800 | R125 599 + 36% above 530 200 |
| 695 801 - 887 000 | R185 215 + 39% above 695 800 |
| 887 001 - 1 878 600 | R259 783 + 41% above 887 000 |
| 1 878 601 and above | R666 339 + 45% above 1 878 600 |

- Rebates deducted from the tax: primary R17 820; secondary (65+) R9 765; tertiary (75+) R3 249 (SARS, same page, as of November 2026).
- Late or underpaid tax carries interest at the SARS Table 1 rate: **10.50% from 1 September 2026** (it was 10.25% from 1 March to 31 August 2026). The rate follows the PFMA rate and changes with a lag, so check the current table. Source: SARS "Interest Rates - Table 1" (September 2026), as of November 2026.

## VAT
Source: SARS VAT page, "Tax periods for VAT".
- Standard rate: **15%**.
- Compulsory registration: taxable supplies above **R2.3 million** (raised from R1 million, effective 1 April 2026; SARS VAT page, as of November 2026).
- Voluntary registration threshold: **R120 000** (raised from R50 000, effective 1 April 2026; same source).
- Vendors charge output VAT on sales and claim input VAT on business purchases; the net goes to SARS.
- Tax periods (SARS "Tax periods for VAT"):

| Category | Period | Typical for |
|---|---|---|
| A | Every 2 months, ending Jan, Mar, May, Jul, Sep, Nov | Standard, assigned by SARS |
| B | Every 2 months, ending Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug, Oct, Dec | Standard, assigned by SARS |
| C | Monthly | Taxable supplies above R30 million, by application, or if assigned for non-compliance |
| D | Every 6 months | Certain farmers (under R1.5m) and micro businesses registered under the Sixth Schedule |
| E | Annual | Certain vendors, e.g. some companies/trusts in letting activities (see VAT 404 guide) |

- Due date: return and payment by the **25th**, or the **last business day** of the month if you file and pay through eFiling, following the end of the tax period; if the 25th is not a business day, the preceding business day. Source: SARS "Obligations of a VAT vendor".
- Example: Category A vendor, period ends 30 November; file and pay by the end of December via eFiling.
- Mistakes: not tracking the rolling 12-month turnover against the threshold; claiming input VAT without valid tax invoices.

## Employer taxes: PAYE, UIF, SDL
- PAYE (employees' tax): the employer deducts income tax from pay and pays SARS.
- **EMP201** monthly declaration and payment: due "within seven days after the end of the month" in which amounts were deducted (the previous business day if the 7th falls on a weekend or holiday). Source: SARS PAYE page.
- **EMP501** reconciliations: interim for 1 March to 31 August 2026 is open 21 September to 31 October 2026; annual for 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027 is open 1 April to 31 May 2027. Source: SARS PAYE page.
- **UIF**: 1% of remuneration deducted from the employee and 1% paid by the employer (SARS summary of the Unemployment Insurance Contributions Act). SARS UIF page (as of November 2026): remuneration ceiling R17 712 per month (R212 544 a year), maximum R177.12 per month each, in force since 1 June 2021 and still the figure SARS publishes. I found no newer official notice; check SARS before relying on it, as an increase has been mooted.
- **SDL** (Skills Development Levy): **1%** of total remuneration; employers whose leviable remuneration over the next 12 months will not exceed **R500 000** are exempt. Source: SARS "Other Taxes" page and Budget 2026 FAQ, as of November 2026.
- Register employer, PAYE, UIF and SDL via SARS; the Compensation Fund (COIDA) is separate and not covered here.
- Mistake: treating a regular "contractor" as non-employee when the relationship is employment.

## Provisional tax
- Who: companies, and individuals with income other than a salary from a PAYE employer (SARS "Provisional tax").
- Instalments (for a year of assessment starting in March):

| Payment | Due |
|---|---|
| First | Within six months of year start: **31 August** (or last business day before it) |
| Second | Last business day of **February** |
| Third (voluntary top-up) | Last business day of September for non-company taxpayers (and companies with February year-ends); otherwise within six months after year-end |

- Filed as IRP6 on eFiling, based on estimated taxable income; underestimating can bring penalties and interest.
- Companies with other year-ends: the same pattern applies relative to their own year.
- Mistake: basing the estimate on last year's profit when the business has grown.

## Dividends tax
- Rate: **20%** on dividends paid on or after 22 February 2017 unless an exemption or treaty rate applies (unchanged in the 2026 Budget). Source: SARS Dividends Tax page and Budget 2026 tax guide, as of November 2026.
- The beneficial owner is liable; the company (withholding agent) withholds and pays SARS by the last day of the month after payment, with DTR01/DTR02 returns.
- Example: R100 000 dividend: R20 000 withheld, shareholder receives R80 000.
- Owner-managers usually combine a salary (taxed via PAYE) and dividends; the best mix depends on personal circumstances, so take advice.

## Turnover tax (micro businesses)
Source: SARS Turnover Tax page.
- Eligibility: annual turnover up to **R2.3 million** (raised effective 1 April 2026; SARS Turnover Tax page, as of November 2026).
- Open to individuals, partnerships, close corporations, companies and co-operatives within the limit.
- It replaces income tax, provisional tax, VAT, capital gains tax and dividends tax for qualifying businesses (VAT is replaced as per SARS wording; check eligibility conditions such as which activities qualify).
- Rates (years of assessment ending 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027):

| Turnover (R) | Tax |
|---|---|
| 0 - 600 000 | 0% |
| 600 001 - 950 000 | 1% above 600 000 |
| 950 001 - 1 400 000 | R3 500 + 2% above 950 000 |
| 1 400 001 and above | R12 500 + 3% above 1 400 000 |

- Payments: first by last business day of August, second by last business day of February, final after the TT03 return is processed; TT03 filing window 1 July to 31 January the following year.
- Example: turnover R1 000 000: 3 500 + 2% x 50 000 = R4 500.
- Mistake: assuming it always saves tax; compare with the ordinary regime, especially for businesses with large costs.

## Record-keeping
- Under section 29 of the Tax Administration Act, keep records that let you meet your tax obligations and let SARS verify them, for **five years** from the date a return is submitted (or from the relevant period if no return is required). Source: SARS short guide to the Tax Administration Act and record-keeping page.
- Longer if an audit, objection or appeal is open.
- Keep originals or an acceptable electronic copy, stored safely and in order, available for inspection.
- Failure to keep records can be a criminal offence and attract administrative penalties.
- Practice: tax invoices, bank statements, payroll records, contracts, asset register, VAT workings.

## B-BBEE basics
- Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment is scored under the Codes of Good Practice (dtic). Relevant when selling to government or large corporates that score their suppliers.
- Size categories by annual **total revenue** (dtic Amended Code Series 000, Statement 000, Government Gazette 42496 of 31 May 2019, paragraphs 4-6; as of November 2026): **EME** R10 million or less, **QSE** between R10 million and R50 million, generic enterprises R50 million and more.
- An EME is deemed a Level 4 contributor (100% recognition). A 100% black-owned EME may be elevated to Level 1 (135%); one at least 51% black-owned to Level 2 (125%). An EME may choose to be measured on the QSE scorecard instead (paras 4.3-4.5).
- An EME needs only a sworn affidavit or a CIPC certificate each year confirming revenue of R10 million or less and the level of black ownership; misrepresentation is a criminal offence (paras 4.6-4.7).
- QSEs are measured on the five elements of the QSE scorecard (ownership, management control, skills development, enterprise and supplier development, socio-economic development).
- Sector codes (construction, finance, marketing, etc.) can set different thresholds; check the code for your sector. Splitting a business to stay under a threshold can be an offence.
- Check thedtic.gov.za for any later amendment of Statement 000.

## POPIA
Source: Information Regulator (inforegulator.org.za).
- The Protection of Personal Information Act governs how a business processes personal information of customers, staff and others.
- Eight conditions for lawful processing: accountability, processing limitation, purpose specification, further processing limitation, information quality, openness, security safeguards, data subject participation.
- Information Officer: each body has one (by default the head of the business). Information Officers and deputies must be registered with the Regulator through its eServices portal.
- Practical steps: list what personal data you hold and why; collect only what you need; secure it; tell people how it is used; have a way to handle access and deletion requests; breach response plan; get consent for direct marketing.
- Mistake: emailing marketing to purchased lists.

## Recall

**Q: At what turnover is VAT registration compulsory, and what can you do below it?**

??? note "Answer"
    Above R2.3 million in taxable supplies (SARS, from 1 April 2026). Voluntary registration is possible above R120 000.

**Q: When is a CIPC annual return due and what must go with it?**

??? note "Answer"
    Within 30 business days after the anniversary of incorporation, with an up-to-date beneficial ownership declaration (filing is blocked without it) and financial statements or the supplement as applicable.

**Q: What do EME and QSE mean in B-BBEE?**

??? note "Answer"
    Exempted Micro Enterprise (turnover up to R10 million) and Qualifying Small Enterprise (above R10 million to R50 million), under the general Codes; sector codes can differ.

**Q: How long must tax records be kept?**

??? note "Answer"
    Five years from the date the return is submitted, longer if an audit or dispute is open.

**Q: Which employer levies apply to payroll, and at what rates?**

??? note "Answer"
    UIF 1% employee plus 1% employer, and SDL 1% unless leviable pay stays under R500 000 a year, both declared monthly on the EMP201.
